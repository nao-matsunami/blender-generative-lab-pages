# Algorithmic Light Objects — current direction, 2026-11-01

The user wants an ongoing family of nonfigurative, algorithmically generated forms that can become 3D-printed lanterns or light objects. **Phase Diagrid** is the accepted visual direction: the construction rule is visible in the finished silhouette. It is a reference point, not a template to repeat.

## Design boundary

- Let a geometric rule create the body: changing lattice density, phase, curvature, fold angle, cell size, layer offset, or facet orientation. Explain the rule and expose meaningful parameters in the generator.
- Do not carry forward the prior organ series' soft mass, anatomical associations, fleshy folds, dark crimson/rose palette, or frontally centered black cavity. A curved surface is acceptable only when its geometric construction is the clear subject.
- Do not make a recognizable shadow picture in this focused series. **Night Orchard** remains a separate, valid shadow-art study; return to that track only when requested.
- The lamp should read as a designed object without the light on. Light should reveal depth and rhythm in the geometry.
- Use neutral mineral white, charcoal, sand, graphite, or restrained metallic materials for presentation. Other colors are possible when they serve a clear geometric idea.

## Variation cycle

Rotate the *generating algorithm and resulting topology*, not just color or the number of apertures. Useful distinct families:

1. **Ruled fold shell:** broad planar strips or origami-like folds joined into a load-bearing open shade.
2. **Contour lamellae:** offset horizontal or vertical slices with changing spacing and twist, connected by structural bridges.
3. **Graded cellular skin:** a polygonal Voronoi or other cell rule with a controlled size gradient and print-safe junctions.
4. **Minimal-surface volume:** a cropped gyroid or related continuous field with a stable rim and intentional thickness.
5. **Interference layers:** two offset screens whose overlap creates moiré as the viewer moves; print and assemble as distinct parts if that is cleaner.
6. **Modular polyhedral system:** repeating units that change orientation or scale through an explicit rule, forming a non-symmetric whole.

Phase Diagrid already uses crossing diagonal bands on a twisted faceted column. The next piece should use another family and change at least three of silhouette, topology, construction method, surface rhythm, material, and camera composition.

## Prototype and review gates

- Work in millimetres. Document intended outer dimensions, nominal wall and narrowest structural member. Use a stable bottom or a documented base interface; leave access for the LED assembly and ventilation.
- Prefer low-heat LED prototypes. A bulb/socket, power circuit, cable routing, thermal clearance, and structural base are separate engineering choices until specified and tested.
- Export STL and GLB. Check one connected component, manifold edge incidence, outward orientation, and a visually legible Blender preview. Run a slicer review and a physical test before calling the design print-ready.
- For daily public work, start Blender GUI and its watcher only when a job requires them, then close Blender after the queue and exports finish, preserving any unsaved work. Follow [gui-job-watcher.md](gui-job-watcher.md). Keep the actual production date, preview inspection, revision render, build, quality, and publish gates. Do not add a second counted daily piece or backfill missed dates.
- Record a remaining weakness in each report. Avoid calling a render-only shade a finished electrical lamp.
